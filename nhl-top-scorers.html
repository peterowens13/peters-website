Build your shot Stick length & flex, fit to you

Uses the same knob-to-chin fitting rule as the rack above (stick ≈ height − 8", worn on skates), plus a common starting-point flex guideline of roughly half your body weight, nudged softer for quick-release shots and stiffer for power shots. These are starting points for a fitting, not a guarantee — get on the ice and adjust from there, and a shop can cut a stick down but can't add length back.

Curve families what each one is actually good for

Deep toe curve

e.g. MacKinnon's P14-style

Opens the blade face for lifting the puck quickly off the toe — good for tight-space snap shots and toe-drags. Trade-off: passes and one-timers off the toe are less predictable.

Max-height / shaved toe

e.g. Caufield's P92

Puts a large, consistent curve low on the blade for a quick, accurate release from anywhere on the blade — built for shooters who get the puck off in one motion.

Squared / closed face

e.g. McDavid's P92 variant

Flatter, more square blade face that's forgiving for receiving passes and handling the puck at speed — a good all-around choice while you're still building a shot.

Mid-curve (P28/P86 family)

e.g. Kucherov

Curve sits in the middle of the blade, balancing backhand control with a reliable forehand release — a versatile pick for a playmaking, pass-first game.

Curve is the piece of equipment most worth trying in person before you buy — grip a few patterns off a store rack and see which one feels like it's already lined up with a natural wrist shot.
